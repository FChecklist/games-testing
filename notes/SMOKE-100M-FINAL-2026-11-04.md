## The answer in five lines
1. **375,600,000 unique scenarios** were run to the end through the real engine code of the six games (target: 100,000,000). Each scenario has its own seed, so any one can be replayed.
2. **Unique vs repeated:** 100,000 scenarios were run more than once (small overlaps, listed in the notes); they are counted once in the unique figure.
3. **No engine crash** in any scenario.
4. Some scenarios broke at least one rule (table below). Most come from a small number of real engine bugs, each hit very often. They are listed in the defect files, with fix branches.
5. Lucky Draw is the thinnest game (private repository, so it cannot run on the cloud sandboxes; it ran on the partner laptop and the phone only).

## What 100 million scenarios do prove
In each of those cases (seeded schedules, claims, pauses and resumes, families, hosts and co-hosts, name and ID cleaning, tie rules, rankings, prize settlements) the engine rules either held, or the failing rule and seed are recorded. A mutation check (the engine deliberately broken in small ways) was caught by the generators in every game, which shows the checks can really fail.

## What they do NOT prove
- That nothing can ever go wrong. They show the rules held in the cases that were generated: many, but finite, and shaped by what I thought to generate.
- Anything outside the engine code: database and HTTP routes, real phones and browsers, screens, payments, e-mail, beacon signatures, real crowds and real network timing. The live rounds (250 and 100 players) belong to the live-rounds session.
- That product behaviour is what the owner wants where the product has no written rule (see the owner-decision list).
- That the fixes hold at scale: they are not yet re-tested against the large scenario set.
